\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
ALT observations are few and clustered; of the models that extend them to unobserved cells and years, a one-coefficient physical model offers transferability and machine learning offers accuracy near its training sites, and learning the residual of a Stefan model retains most of both. Evaluated on \nncyAll{} site-year observations of three regions with the climate of each observation year, a gradient-boosting model of the residual of a locally recalibrated Stefan model reduced the error of that model by \ndrefLeblkRoabs{} cm in the Lena Delta and \ndrefCablkRoabs{} cm in Canada under spatial-block validation. Its gain was resolved in all three regions with 100 local labels, and with 25 labels it was at least as accurate as the Stefan model. With the Lena Delta withheld from training it stayed within \ndrefLeregRoabs{} cm of the Stefan model, whereas the error of direct ML exceeded that of the Stefan model by \ndrefLeregDzabs{} cm. On the same sites and years, the published ML products matched this model only near their own training sites and did not represent the thin active layers of the Lena Delta. The workflow addresses a limitation of each existing approach: it adds to the Stefan model the within-region variation that one coefficient cannot represent, it gives machine learning a physical term that limits the error increase away from the training sites, and its evaluation protocol tests products at stated sites and years outside their training data. The yearly 1~km maps for 2011--2024, with an area of applicability and prediction intervals, provide year-specific thaw depths for ground-stability, hydrological and carbon applications, and in regions with few observations the workflow provides a Stefan-based starting map that improves when local labels represent the region. Daily climate inputs, remotely sensed snow and soil moisture, a moisture-aware anchor and labels from further regions could improve the accuracy and coverage of the maps.
