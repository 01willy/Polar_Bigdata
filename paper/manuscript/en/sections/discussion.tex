\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The results define a label-count workflow for a new permafrost region (Fig.~7d). Without target labels, the reference is the source-coefficient (or year-matched) Stefan model, and ML enters only through physics pseudo-labels or a low-weight residual. With three to ten labels, the coefficient is recalibrated (lower error in one of four regions) and a low-weight residual added. From 40 labels, the method is selected by cross-validation within the target labels, with the gain arising in Canada. In this pool, the methods recommended from 3 to 160 labels had higher point-estimate error than the source-coefficient model [DECISION: Fig 7d 경로의 y 기준과 풀, 그림 명세 D-13]. Within regions with thousands of labels, residual ML follows, with its gain between climate grid cells (Fig.~7a--c). Placement enters only as a pre-specified procedure, as spreading labels lowered error in Canada but not in the Lena Delta (Fig.~5).

The gains came from correcting the level of the Stefan coefficient $E$ and bias at the climate-grid scale. $E$ lumps soil thermal conductivity, the n-factor and the latent heat of soil water and ice~\cite{Riseborough2008}, which is why regional Stefan maps used land-cover-specific coefficients~\cite{Nelson1997,ShiklomanovNelson2002}; here it also absorbs thawing-index bias and the label definition (Supplementary Note 1). With all labels, recalibration made up 85\% of the four-region mean reduction by residual ML relative to the source coefficient, a mean dominated by W Russia (recalibration 13.5 of 14.0~cm; post hoc arithmetic). The regional coefficient of Canada was the closest of the main regions to the source value but varied between label and scoring blocks; ten-label recalibration increased error there, and the selection rule mostly chose a residual weight of 1.0, which we read as correcting label-location heterogeneity.

The zero-label results agree in direction with an Alaskan random-split comparison in which a random forest generalized less well than the Stefan model~\cite{Gautam2025}. An earlier within-Alaska analysis [DECISION: 대회 보고서 인용 여부] found a larger gain of a physics residual over the Stefan model, as the best of 56 settings, than the stricter test here (Supplementary Note 2). kNNDM~\cite{Linnenbrink2024} only mimics map-to-label distances in this clustered sample (13,606 Alaskan rows at 343 locations).

Alternative explanations are unlikely. Stefan pseudo-labels had lower error than shuffled and constant pseudo-labels (Fig.~3b), whereas a linear thawing-index placebo differed from them by less than 0.5~cm, so the gain reflects cell-level thawing-index information more than the square-root form. Recalibration gains held with single-year labels in W and E Russia and were 0.86~cm smaller with point labels than with 1~km location means in Canada (median, ten labels), which sets the label unit of the workflow. Physics and CatBoost results agreed between computing environments (maximum difference $3.6\times10^{-14}$~cm) and a neural network differed by up to 5.6~cm, so the workflow uses only these deterministic methods.

The workflow does not rank sites for measurement or predict variation within climate grid cells; the 1~km maps (Supplementary Figs~S10--S14) are a display resolution, and their information resolution is bounded by the climate (about 10~km) and soil (about 5~km) inputs. The conclusions are restricted to source data that include ground-penetrating radar labels, and climates warmer than the training range could not be evaluated. The Alaskan dominance of the source data (78--94\% of source cells) matters most at small label counts, because the source coefficient carries the weight of ten labels in the recalibrated coefficient. Confirmatory evidence comes from four re-used main regions and one new shallow region; the unblinded Tibetan Plateau is the only deep-regime region and is reported separately, so results are not generalized beyond these regions. Values depend on the test design (Fig.~1e), so randomly split studies are not directly comparable. Soil moisture, organic-layer thickness, snow duration and microtopography within grid cells are nearly absent from the covariates, so the within-grid share explained in Alaska (below 1\%) describes these inputs, not a physical limit. Method selection, placement, the label-rich tests, the bias correlation and the gain decomposition were designed after earlier results were viewed and are weaker evidence than the internally pre-registered contrasts.

Each step carries a condition: labels are 1~km location means, recalibration rests on the ten-label contrast, selection by cross-validation within the target labels depends on the number of 0.5$^{\circ}$ blocks covered by the labels and increased error relative to the fixed recipe in the Lena Delta with ten labels, and the whole workflow was tested only in new random splits of the same regions, with its placement reduced to random sampling and its non-inferiority to the recalibrated Stefan model holding for the pooled mean but not in E Russia, Alaska or the Lena Delta.
