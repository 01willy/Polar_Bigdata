\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Evaluation design and validation ladder}\label{sec:results-design}

On the holdout protocol described above (Fig.~1c,d; Table~1), every method was compared with two Stefan baselines that used the same target labels, as in lake-temperature studies\cite{Read2019,Jia2021}: the source-coefficient Stefan model, whose coefficient $E_0$ was estimated from the source cells (Table~1), and the Stefan model recalibrated with the target labels by shrinkage towards $E_0$ ($\kappa = 10$)\cite{Steyerberg2004}.
Four-region means weight the Lena Delta, Canada, W Russia and E Russia equally and exclude Alaska, which is a held-out reference target.
Intervals are cell-weighted unless marked block-equal.

Averaged over five regions with equal weight, the RMSE of direct ML increased from 22.9~cm under random cell splits to 33.3~cm under region holdout (difference 10.3~cm; 95\% confidence interval (CI) 7.3 to 12.8; block-equal 6.1~cm, 4.7 to 7.6), whereas a Stefan model with the coefficient fitted by least squares to the training cells stayed between 26.8 and 27.0~cm (Fig.~1e).
The 10.3~cm increase is reported as an effect size rather than a confirmatory result, and the registered contrast between cluster holdout with a 500~km buffer and random cells was 8.2~cm (95\% CI 5.6 to 10.4; Supplementary Table S2).
Moving from random-cell to $k$-fold nearest-neighbour distance matching (kNNDM) splits\cite{Linnenbrink2024} increased the RMSE of direct ML by 5.76, 8.17 and 14.20~cm and that of the Stefan model fitted to the training cells by 0.29, 0.51 and 4.28~cm in Alaska, the Lena Delta and Canada (cell-weighted; descriptive, designed after earlier results were viewed; Fig.~1e; Supplementary Table S13).
Random validation has been argued to estimate map accuracy validly for probability samples\cite{Wadoux2021}, but our labels are a clustered non-probability sample, so the errors below are prediction errors on held-out blocks.

\subsection{Transfer without target labels}\label{sec:results-label0}

Without target labels, pseudo-labels from the Stefan model reduced RMSE relative to shuffled pseudo-labels by 1.6~cm (95\% CI 1.0 to 2.0; block-equal 0.6 to 1.5; four regions; Holm-adjusted \textit{P} = 0.001), and in a post hoc count, fewer of 30 targets lost more than 2~cm against the source-coefficient Stefan model with physics pseudo-label augmentation (2 targets) than with direct ML (18 targets) (Fig.~3b; Fig.~6a,b).
Against two constant placebos the reductions were 1.7 and 3.6~cm, and against a placebo linear in $\sqrt{\mathrm{TDD}}$ 0.48~cm (registered verdict: mixed).
The 2~cm threshold was set after the transfer results had been viewed, the 30 targets include 23 sub-regions or label-expanded versions, and counts based on intervals give the same order (post hoc; Supplementary Table S5).

For the five learners whose primary and common-resampling intervals agreed, direct ML had higher four-region mean error than the source-coefficient Stefan model (Fig.~6c).
For the main learner, CatBoost, the difference was 2.2~cm (95\% CI 1.1 to 3.4; Holm-adjusted \textit{P} = 0.023), and for two further CatBoost settings, TabPFN v2 and TabICL v2 it was 0.85 to 1.9~cm.
These five verdicts depend on the split-independence assumption, and the TabPFN v2 verdict was significant before correction only (Holm-adjusted \textit{P} = 0.117).
Residual models with a low weight ($\lambda = 0.25$) on the source-coefficient anchor were equivalent to that model within ±0.5~cm for three learners (depends on the split-independence assumption).

With Alaska held out, physics pseudo-labels had 1.8~cm higher error than the source-coefficient model (95\% CI 0.4 to 2.6).
On the Tibetan Plateau, where the source coefficient is far from the regional value (Fig.~1b), direct ML had 60.0~cm lower error (an expected result from an unblinded, cross-environment comparison).
With ten labels, direct TabICL v2 had 2.9~cm lower four-region mean error (95\% CI 1.1 to 3.6; depends on the split-independence assumption) but 6.0~cm higher error in the three-region mean that includes Alaska.

On the same blocks, after masking blocks within 5 or 25~km of product training sites (registration deviation; designed after earlier results were viewed), the existing CCI v5 map without labels had 23.79~cm higher error than the source-coefficient Stefan model (Holm-adjusted \textit{P} = 0.0006; higher in the Lena Delta, Canada and E Russia), and residual ML had 8.37 and 7.51~cm lower error than CCI v5 recalibrated with the same 10 and all labels (CCI v5 contrasts partly unblinded); no difference was established for the Wei v2 map (47.4\% of product values imputed), trained partly on CALM sites in the target regions (ALT definitions differ; Supplementary Table S13).

\subsection{Method selection with tens to hundreds of labels}\label{sec:results-selection}

Selecting the method by five-fold block cross-validation within the target labels reduced RMSE relative to a fixed residual recipe ($\lambda = 0.25$) by 1.3~cm with 160 labels (95\% CI 0.7 to 1.7; block-equal 0.8 to 1.8; Lena Delta and Canada; unchanged under common resampling) and was non-inferior at 40 and 160 labels (margin 0.5~cm), with both results arising in Canada; this rule was designed after the label-grid results had been viewed (Fig.~4c; Fig.~7d).
With 40 labels, the reduction of 0.87~cm (95\% CI 0.27 to 1.20) depends on the split-independence assumption.
In the Lena Delta no difference was established (Fig.~4c).
With ten labels in the four main regions, non-inferiority was not established, and the rule had 0.70~cm higher error in the Lena Delta; with all labels, non-inferiority depends on the split-independence assumption.
The rule did not increase the number of targets with lower error than the recalibrated Stefan model (Supplementary Table S8).

In Canada, no difference from the source-coefficient Stefan model was established (Supplementary Table S8).
With Alaska held out, the rule had higher error than the source-coefficient model with 10, 40 and 160 labels (descriptive curve contrasts; Supplementary Table S8).

At 40 and 160 labels, residual ML had 1.9 to 2.4~cm higher error than physics-input ML in the Lena Delta and Canada (the 2.4~cm contrast depends on the split-independence assumption) (Fig.~3c).
This difference arose in Canada; with Alaska held out residual ML had lower error.
At these label counts no difference was established between residual ML and a stronger recalibrated baseline (the soil-property Stefan model recalibrated with $\kappa = 10$).

Leave-one-source-region-out tuning of neural networks gave learner-specific results, no learner met the registered condition for a learner-general statement, and this tuning was not compared with selection within the target labels (Supplementary Table S8).
In new random splits of the same regions (re-test in reused regions, designed after earlier results were viewed, partly unblinded), the sequential workflow had 0.93~cm lower error than random placement with the fixed residual recipe at 160 labels (Lena Delta and Canada; Canada −2.41~cm, Lena Delta +0.54~cm, undetermined) and no established difference at 40 labels (minimum detectable effect about 0.5~cm; Lena Delta +0.21~cm); because its placement step reduced to random sampling, this compares the method rule with the fixed recipe on the same labels.
In these splits the workflow was non-inferior to the recalibrated Stefan model at 10, 40 and 160 labels (five-region mean, then Lena Delta and Canada), with 1.35 and 1.51~cm lower error at 40 and 160 labels, but only for the pooled mean: it was more than 0.5~cm worse in E Russia at 10 labels (+1.34~cm), in Alaska, the selection family, at 40 and 160 labels (+1.51 and +2.34~cm) and in the Lena Delta at 160 labels (+0.53~cm) (Fig.~7e; Supplementary Table S13).
Within regions (auxiliary arm), the rule was equivalent to residual ML at 200 labels, with no difference established at 500 and 1000 labels.
Stacking also established no difference at 40 and 160 labels (Lena Delta and Canada; designed after earlier results were viewed, partly unblinded; Supplementary Table S13).
