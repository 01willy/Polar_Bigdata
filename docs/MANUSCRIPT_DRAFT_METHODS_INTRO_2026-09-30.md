## Introduction

Active-layer thickness (ALT) is the maximum seasonal thaw depth of the ground above permafrost. It sets the depth of the biologically active soil and affects infrastructure on thawing ground. Permafrost temperature has increased at monitored boreholes across the Northern Hemisphere (Biskaborn et al., 2019). ALT is measured by probing, thaw tubes, soil pits and ground-penetrating radar (GPR) at a limited number of sites. The GTN-P/CALM compilation contains 263 ALT time series from 1990 to 2024 (Streletskiy et al., 2025). In the cell-level data set used here, the number of labelled cells per region ranges from 3 in Greenland to 13,606 in Alaska (Table 1). ALT maps for most of the permafrost region therefore rely on models fitted in one region and applied in another.

The Stefan solution scales ALT with the square root of the thawing degree-days (TDD) through an edaphic coefficient E that is calibrated against local measurements (Nelson et al., 1997). Statistical and machine-learning (ML) models regress ALT on climate, terrain, soil and satellite-derived covariates (Aalto et al., 2018; Karjalainen et al., 2019; Ran et al., 2022a). Hybrid designs combine both approaches. Physics-model outputs have served as ML input features (Pilyugina et al., 2025 [verify]; Wang et al., 2025). The coefficient E has been predicted from covariates with ML (Ran et al., 2022b; Zhang et al., 2024). Labels generated by physical rules have served as training targets for zero-curtain detection (Gay et al., 2026). Process-model pre-training, residual learning and comparison with recalibrated process models have been evaluated for lake temperature (Read et al., 2019; Jia et al., 2021; Willard et al., 2022). Each of these components has precedents, and this study does not claim methodological novelty for any of them.

The evaluation designs in the ALT literature differ from the situation of a new mapping region. Gautam et al. (2025) compared a random forest with a Stefan model at 68 CALM sites in Alaska with one random 70/30 split and reported test coefficients of determination of 0.24 and 0.54, respectively. Distance-blocked validation has been used for circum-Arctic ALT maps (Aalto et al., 2018; Karjalainen et al., 2019; Ran et al., 2022a). These designs test within-region accuracy or extrapolation without target labels. A mapping project in a new region usually has between zero and a few hundred local measurements. For such a project, the relevant quantity is the error in the target region as a function of the number n of target labels, relative to the Stefan model recalibrated with the same labels. Evaluations of this type exist for unmonitored lakes and ungauged catchments (Pool et al., 2019; Willard et al., 2021; Feng et al., 2023), for subsurface temperature (O'Malley et al., 2026) and for spatial prediction in general (Portes et al., 2026). To our knowledge, no study mapping permafrost ALT or mean annual ground temperature has reported prediction error for regions excluded from the source training data as a function of n.

This study reports such an evaluation for ALT. Each target region is removed from the source data together with a 100 km buffer. Labels are drawn under a fixed rule from one half of the target's 0.5° blocks, and all methods are scored on the other half, for n ∈ {0, 3, 10, 40, 160, 320, 1000, all available}. Following Read et al. (2019) and Jia et al. (2021), every method is compared with two Stefan baselines. Baseline P0 uses the coefficient estimated in the source regions. Baseline P1 recalibrates the coefficient with the same n target labels, with shrinkage toward the source value. Recalibration with few labels can itself increase error (Steyerberg et al., 2004), so differences are reported against both baselines. A gain is attributed to a learner only when it holds against the better of the two.

The methods cover direct ML without a physics anchor, augmentation with Stefan-derived pseudo-labels, Stefan anchors with residual learning and covariate-dependent coefficients. The learners include gradient boosting, default and source-tuned neural networks, generative models and two tabular foundation models. Prediction intervals are evaluated by interval score and empirical coverage in held-out regions. Three regions with direct measurements (the Tibetan Plateau, a North Atlantic group and central Siberia) were added to the four main regions of earlier work. All comparisons use block-bootstrap confidence intervals under two weighting conventions and a four-way verdict with a 0.5 cm equivalence margin.

The hypotheses, contrasts, decision rules and sentence templates were committed to the project repository before the corresponding results were retrieved or opened (internal pre-registration; commit hashes and times in Methods). Negative and indeterminate outcomes are reported under the same rules as positive ones. The contribution is this evaluation design and the registered results it produces, including negative results. ### Observational data

**Labels.** ALT labels of the main regions come from three compilations: GTN-P/CALM (Streletskiy et al., 2025), the ALLena thaw-depth compilation for the Lena River Delta (Veremeeva et al., 2025) and the ABoVE soil moisture and ALT data set for Alaska and Canada, version 2 (Moore et al., 2025). Records were aggregated to cells before modelling. For ABoVE and ALLena, a cell is a unique coordinate rounded to four decimal places, and the label is the mean of all observations at that coordinate. For CALM sites, a cell is one site and the label is the multi-year mean. ABoVE records were retained when 0 < ALT < 300 cm [verify: range rules of the CALM and ALLena parsers]. The version-3 cell table contains 17,572 rows, of which the analysis uses the 17,467 cells with direct labels. In version 3, direct labels are mechanical probing and GPR. CALM sites whose event metadata name thaw tubes or ground temperature were moved to a separate class and excluded. Twenty cells that the source event metadata identify as temperature-derived remained in the direct class (15 in Alaska, 4 in Canada, 1 on the Tibetan Plateau). The table was frozen before this was found. These cells are flagged in the version-4 label table and are handled like the probe-only sensitivity variant. Most Alaskan labels are GPR-derived [old result, recheck: 86 % of Alaskan cells in the audit of 21 September 2026]. Label years span 1990 to 2024 and are not matched to the covariate reference period. The resulting maps are static and refer to the 2015–2020 climatology.

**Regions.** Cells are assigned to macro-regions defined geographically before the analysis. The regions and their direct-label cell counts are Alaska (13,606 cells, 74 blocks of 0.5°), Lena River Delta (3,037 cells, 20 blocks), Canada (750 cells, 39 blocks), Russia W (31 cells, 21 blocks), Russia E (30 cells, 21 blocks), Russia C (7 cells, 6 blocks) and Greenland (3 cells, 2 blocks), with three further cells in other regions. Sub-regions of Alaska (AL-1 to AL-6), Canada (CA-2, CA-3) and the Lena River Delta (LE-1, LE-2) were defined by k-means clustering of 0.5° block centroids without labels. The block-to-sub-region assignment (133 blocks) was fixed in a table before the main run so that it does not depend on the execution environment.

**Label unit.** A label row has a different meaning in different regions. In Russia W and Russia E a row is the multi-year mean of one CALM site. In the Lena River Delta a row is a point observation at a rounded coordinate, usually from a single visit. In Alaska and Canada most rows are point observations and some are CALM site means. In the additional regions (below) a row is the mean over an approximately 1 km cell. The label count n in all figures and tables is the number of rows drawn. Table 1 reports for each region the number of rows, the number of distinct 1 km locations, the number of distinct ERA5-Land climate values and the number of 0.5° blocks. Statements that compare regions at equal n state the unit.

**Covariates.** The input set x25 contains 25 covariates that are available in all regions. Terrain (6): elevation, slope, sine and cosine of aspect, topographic position index and roughness, computed in 33 × 33 pixel windows of the Copernicus DEM GLO-30. Climate (8): mean annual air temperature, TDD, freezing degree-days, √TDD, warmest- and coldest-month temperature, level-1 soil temperature and snow water equivalent from ERA5-Land monthly means averaged over 2015–2020 (Muñoz-Sabater et al., 2021). Coastal cells without a land pixel take the nearest land value within 0.2°, then within 0.5°. Soil (9): clay, sand, silt, bulk density, coarse fragments and pH at 5–15 cm and soil organic carbon at 0–5, 5–15 and 15–30 cm from SoilGrids 2.0 (Poggio et al., 2021). Model-based product (2): the 1997–2021 mean of the ESA CCI Permafrost ALT product version 4.0 and its validity flag (Westermann et al., 2024). The CCI product is output of the CryoGrid model forced partly by the same reanalysis, and it is treated as a model-based covariate, not as an independent observation. TDD and the Stefan solution (Stefan, 1891 [verify]) are

TDD = Σ_m max(T̄_m, 0) d_m,  ALT = E √TDD,  (1)

where T̄_m is the monthly mean 2 m air temperature and d_m is the number of days in month m. Soil thawing degree-days computed from the level-1 soil temperature are used for the scoring mask and for one anchor variant. Label-derived quantities (standard deviation, count and years of observations) are excluded from all inputs, and unit tests enforce this.

**Scoring mask.** A cell is scored only if its label, its CCI ALT value and its soil thawing degree-days are valid. The same scoring cells are used for all methods.

### Additional independent regions (LGD)

Additional labels were collected from public archives for regions at least 100 km from existing labelled regions (Supplementary Table S-LGD). The label rules were fixed before any model was fitted on these regions. A label enters as a target label only if it is a direct measurement (probe, thaw tube, frost tube, GPR, or soil pit, core or auger) of the end-of-season thaw depth. The end of season is established by the data protocol (CALM, thaw tubes), by the maximum over repeated visits after 1 August, by an observation date in August or September for single visits, or by an explicit statement in the data description. Thaw tubes therefore count as direct measurements in the additional regions, whereas CALM thaw-tube sites were excluded in version 3 [verify: harmonize the thaw-tube rule before the Results section]. Values estimated from degree-day ratios or models are not used. Disturbed sites (burned areas, water bodies, thermo-erosional landforms) and censored probe readings are excluded. Only annual values from 1990 onward with 0 < ALT ≤ 600 cm are used. New records are aggregated to approximately 1 km cells, indexed by ky = ⌊lat/0.009⌋ and kx = ⌊lon·cos φ/0.009⌋ with φ = (ky + 0.5)·0.009°. New cells within 0.01° of an existing direct-label cell are removed.

Three target regions were defined geographically: Tibet (Tibetan Plateau and Qilian Mountains, 26–40°N, 73–105°E), NAtlantic (Greenland, Svalbard and mainland Norway, Sweden and Finland) and Russia_C (Russia between 90°E and 140°E, excluding the Lena River Delta window 71.5–73.6°N, 123.3–130.1°E). The North Atlantic grouping was fixed before any result because none of its parts was expected to meet the block criterion alone. Extended versions of Russia W, Russia E and Canada were built as sensitivity cases. A region is eligible for confidence intervals if at least one split is valid and the union of scoring blocks over the used splits contains at least 8 blocks. The regime is shallow if the mean ALT of the target cells is below 150 cm and deep otherwise. Eligibility and regime were determined from the split structure before any model was fitted on these regions. They were recorded with the hash of the input table (18,088 rows = 17,572 version-3 rows + 516 new rows; sha256 4c387b2b…). Tibet (132 cells, 34 blocks, deep regime), NAtlantic (41 cells, 13 blocks, shallow) and Russia_C (57 cells, 13 blocks, shallow) are eligible. Some NAtlantic splits have only three scoring blocks, and NAtlantic results carry a few-blocks flag. All Tibetan target cells lie above the source-cell elevation range. Contrasts against P0 in Tibet are therefore labelled as predicted outcomes and are not interpreted. Temperature-derived labels in Tibet (39 cells) are used only to test the effect of the label definition (L39).

Three pools are compared. P4 contains the four main regions (version-3 cells, main-run shards). PE1 adds the eligible shallow new regions, and PE2 adds the deep region. ### Physics baselines

The source set S of a target contains all direct-label cells outside the target macro-region and outside a 100 km buffer around the target cells. The source coefficient is the least-squares estimate

E0 = Σ_{i∈S} s_i y_i / Σ_{i∈S} s_i².  (2)

Let L_n be the n labels drawn from half A of the target and E_ls,n the least-squares estimate from L_n alone. The recalibrated coefficient shrinks E_ls,n toward E0:

E_n = (n E_ls,n + κ E0) / (n + κ), κ = 10.  (3)

The value κ = 10 was fixed before the main experiment on the basis of earlier experiments, so it is not blind. κ ∈ {3, 30} and a leave-one-target-out selection of κ are reported as sensitivity cases (L28). Four physics methods are defined. P0 predicts E0 s. P1 predicts E_n s. P2 predicts E_ls,n s without shrinkage. P3 predicts exp(m_n) s, where m_n is the posterior mean of log E in a conjugate normal model. Its prior is centred on log E0, with a between-region variance estimated by the method of moments from source regions with at least 3 cells. Its residual variance is pooled within source regions. At n = 0, P1, P2 and P3 equal P0 by definition. P0 is the deployment baseline without target labels. P1 is the baseline for the net value of a learner, because it uses exactly the same labels.

### Learning methods

All learning methods use the x25 inputs. Training rows are the source cells S together with the n drawn target labels, with equal row weight (α = 1) unless stated otherwise. The anchored methods have the form

ŷ(x) = a(x) + λ g(c(x)),  (4)

where a(x) is a physics anchor and g is trained on residuals.

| id | Method | Definition | Reference λ |
|---|---|---|---|
| D0 | Direct ML | y regressed on x25. No physics anchor; the inputs include √TDD and the CCI ALT product | 1.0 |
| D1 | Augmented direct ML | D0 plus pseudo-label rows E_n s for cells of half A, sampled with replacement. The number of pseudo-label rows is r = 10 times the number of source rows. Drawn label cells keep their observed value | 1.0 |
| R0 | Source-coefficient anchor with residual | a = E0 s; g trained on y − E0 s for source and target rows | 0.25 |
| R1 | Recalibrated anchor with residual | a = E_n s; g trained on y − E0 s for source rows and on y − E_n s for target rows. R1 = R0 at n = 0 | 0.25 |
| R2 | Augmentation, anchor and residual | R1 plus pseudo-residual rows with value 0 for half-A cells (r = 10) | 0.25 |
| R3 | Stacked | D1 prediction as anchor; g trained on the residual of D1 | 0.25 |
| V1 | Covariate-dependent coefficient | z = log(y/s) regressed on standardized x25 by ridge regression (penalty 10) on source and target rows; a = exp(ẑ) s with ẑ clipped to the training range | none |
| V1r | V1 with residual | V1 anchor plus λ g | 0.25 |

The residual weight λ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1.0} is applied after fitting to the same model. Verdicts use one reference λ per method, fixed before the main run (table). The learner of the method axis is CatBoost (Prokhorenkova et al., 2018) with 200 iterations, learning rate 0.05, depth 3 and L2 regularization 3, run with two random seeds.

Three further axes vary one factor at a time. The target-weight axis gives target rows the weight α ∈ {1, 10, 100} or continues the training of the source model on target rows only. It is applied to D0, R0 and R1 for n ≥ 3. A nested selection of α by block cross-validation inside half A is evaluated for four eligible targets (L3). The placement axis compares cell-random draws with block-spread draws, which cycle through all blocks of half A, for P1 and R1 at n ∈ {10, 40, 160}. The learner axis applies ridge regression and seven neural or generative learners to D0, R1 and R2 at n ∈ {0, 10, 40, 160, all}. It covers 12 targets (the seven version-3 main-region targets including Alaska, AL-1 to AL-3 and CA-2 and CA-3 in mode x), 3 splits, 2 draws and 2 seeds. The learners are a multilayer perceptron (25–256–128–64–1 units, ReLU, batch normalization, dropout 0.1), RealMLP with the default settings of pytabkit 1.7.3 (Holzmüller et al., 2024), a reduced FT-Transformer-type model (token dimension 64, 8 heads, 3 blocks; Gorishniy et al., 2021), a multi-head MLP with 8 independent heads, conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023), a denoising diffusion model with 200 steps (Ho et al., 2020) and a conditional rational-quadratic spline normalizing flow (Durkan et al., 2019). The multi-head MLP is named `tabm` in the code but is not the TabM architecture of Gorishniy et al. (2025). Point predictions of generative learners are the median of 64 samples per cell. Neural learners use Adam, a batch size of 8,192 rows, at most 100 epochs and early stopping on a random 10 % of training rows. These settings were not tuned. Tuned versions are evaluated separately (LGF).

### Targets, splits and label draws

A target is a region in a source mode. In mode x the source excludes the target macro-region and a 100 km buffer, and for a sub-region it also excludes the parent region. In mode i a sub-region keeps the rest of its parent region in the source, so that it serves as a simulated new region inside a labelled region. The 27 targets are the six version-3 main regions (Lena River Delta, Canada, Russia W, Russia E, Russia C, Greenland) in mode x, Alaska as a whole in mode x, AL-1 to AL-6 in modes i and x, and CA-2, CA-3, LE-1 and LE-2 in modes i and x. Russia C and Greenland have too few blocks in version 3 for confidence intervals and are reported as point estimates.

Each target is split into halves A and B five times. For each split, the 0.5° blocks of the target are randomly permuted and assigned one by one to the half with fewer cells (split seeds 1–5). Block-wise separation of labelled and scored cells follows common practice for spatially structured data (Roberts et al., 2017). Duplicate splits are dropped, and splits with fewer than two scoring blocks are invalid. Labels are drawn from half A, and scoring uses the masked cells of half B. For each n in {0, 3, 10, 40, 160, 320, 1000} smaller than the number of cells in A, and for n equal to all cells of A, five draws of n cells are made at random without replacement. The random seed of a draw is fixed by the target, mode, split, n and draw number. Every method, learner and weight setting therefore uses the same labels. Russia W and Russia E have 14–17 cells in A, so their largest grid value is n = 10, in addition to the full set.

### Scoring and statistical inference

For a method, let SSE_b and N_b be the sum of squared errors and the number of scoring cells in block b of half B. Two root-mean-square errors are computed:

RMSE_cell = ( Σ_b SSE_b / Σ_b N_b )^{1/2},  RMSE_block = (1/|B|) Σ_b (SSE_b / N_b)^{1/2}.  (5)

The cell-weighted value is primary. The block-weighted value limits the influence of densely sampled blocks. The paired difference of two methods A and B is

Δ(A − B) = RMSE(A) − RMSE(B)  (cm).  (6)

It is computed for the same split, draw and seed, averaged over seeds and draws within a split and then averaged over splits. Negative values favour A.

Confidence intervals are obtained by a block bootstrap within each split. In each replicate the scoring blocks of half B are resampled with replacement, and the same block multiplicities are applied to both methods and to all draws and seeds. Replicate distributions are averaged across splits by replicate index, and the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles give the 95 % interval. Hypotheses L1–L8 of LG use 1,000 replicates. The extension experiments and the abstract bundle use 10,000 replicates, because Holm-adjusted p values need a finer resolution. A stratified mean gives each of the four main regions equal weight and combines the regions replicate by replicate. A region enters a pooled interval if it has at least one valid split and at least 8 scoring blocks in the union over splits. When fewer than four regions qualify, the verdict carries the label "partial (k/4 regions)", and with fewer than two regions it is not determined. At n ∈ {40, 160} the pooled mean contains only the Lena River Delta and Canada. An auxiliary interval draws one set of block multiplicities for the union of a region's scoring blocks and applies it to all splits. If its verdict differs from that of the primary interval, the result is labelled as dependent on the split-independence assumption.

**Four-way verdict.** A contrast is superior if the upper limits of both the cell-weighted and the block-weighted intervals are below 0. It is inferior if both lower limits are above 0. It is equivalent if it is neither and all four interval limits lie within ±δ, which corresponds to two one-sided tests at the 2.5 % level (Schuirmann, 1987). Otherwise it is indeterminate. The primary margin is δ = 0.5 cm, and δ = 1.0 cm and δ = 0.02 × RMSE(P0) are sensitivity margins. Results that depend on the margin are labelled. For contrasts between two methods that both use λ, equivalence is stated only if it holds at the reference λ and at λ = 1.0. A superior or inferior contrast with |Δ| < 0.5 cm is reported as statistically distinguishable with an effect below 0.5 cm. The margins were fixed before any result and were not changed afterwards. After fixing them, we checked that 92 % of the ABoVE probe values are whole centimetres, which indicates a reading unit of about 1 cm. The two margins correspond to about 1–5 % of the source-coefficient Stefan RMSE in the main regions [old result, recheck: P0 RMSE 21.6–42.5 cm under the protocol of 21 September 2026]. The margins are conventions and are not derived from measurement error. An interval that includes zero is not described as showing no difference.

**Minimum label count and label net value.** For each method, the minimum label count n* is the smallest n at which the contrast with P0 is superior, the share of splits with Δ < 0 is at least 2/3 and the share of draws with Δ < 0 is at least 0.75. When n* lies between grid points it is reported as an interval, for example more than 40 and at most 160. Every Δ at n > 0 is reported together with the label net value Δ(n) − Δ(0) of the same method.

**Region-level inference.** The pooled intervals are conditional on the four regions. For inference about regions in general, key contrasts are recomputed over 50 splits (200 splits for analytic methods) in the four main regions and Alaska. They are summarized by the number of regions with negative Δ, an exact sign test, a sign-flip permutation test, a Hartung-Knapp random-effects mean (Hartung and Knapp, 2001) and leave-one-region-out means (LGX-N4). A statement about regions in general is made only if the Hartung-Knapp interval excludes zero. With four regions, the smallest attainable two-sided sign-test p value is 0.125.

**Independent and dependent targets.** The four main regions in mode x and the eligible LGD regions are treated as independent targets, and confidence intervals and verdict words are reported for them. The main regions were used in earlier experiments of this project. Their verdicts are therefore labelled as re-tests of reused regions, and verdicts in the LGD regions are labelled as independent-region confirmation. Alaska in mode x is a reference target and is not averaged with the main regions. An auxiliary three-region mean (Lena River Delta, Canada and Alaska) is reported. Sub-region targets share scoring or source cells with their parent region. Their results are reported as counts of targets in each verdict class, and pooled means over sub-regions are not given confidence intervals in the main text.

**Abstract contrast bundle.** Verdict words in the abstract are restricted to ten contrasts fixed before the results (Table 3). Two-sided bootstrap p values are computed from the cell-weighted and the block-weighted distributions of the same 10,000 replicates, and the larger p is used. Holm's procedure (Holm, 1979) is applied to the ten p values with the family size fixed at 10. A missing or undetermined contrast enters with p = 1 and is not replaced. Equivalence p values, max(P(Δ* ≤ −δ), P(Δ* ≥ δ)) taken over the two weightings, are Holm-adjusted as a separate family. In the abstract, a superior or inferior contrast is stated with its direction only if its adjusted p is below 0.05. Otherwise the abstract states that a difference was not established. An equivalent contrast is stated with its margin only if equivalence holds after adjustment. These rules govern the abstract wording only. Hypothesis verdicts follow the rules of each registration.

### Extension contrasts (LGX)

The extension refits the eight base methods with the same splits, draws and seeds, so that every extension contrast is closed within its own shards. A reproduction check compares the refitted base methods with the main-run shards (per-key cell-weighted RMSE; tolerance 1e-9 cm for physics methods and 0.02 cm for CatBoost methods). The extension covers physics outputs as input features (F1a, F1k, F1n), log-ratio regression (V2) and multiplicative correction (RM). It covers pseudo-label controls: shuffled, target-level constant, source-level constant and linear-TDD pseudo-labels, pseudo-label ratios r ∈ {1, 3, 30} and shuffled target labels. It covers a validation ladder on the pooled data: random cells, coarse covariate groups, 0.05° cells, 0.5° blocks, leave-one-cluster-out with 0, 100 and 500 km buffers, and region holdout. It also covers within-region block cross-validation, a proximity ladder at fixed scoring cells, cell-level predictions with distance strata, learner equivalence over five splits and a physics-baseline ladder (affine Stefan, a Kudryavtsev-type model (Kudryavtsev et al., 1974 [verify]), a soil-property Stefan variant, the CCI ALT product and their ensemble). Finally, it covers an error floor from within-group variance, learner capacity (CatBoost with 600 iterations and depth 6, a random forest with 500 trees, and CatBoost tuned by leave-one-source-region-out validation) and sensitivity variants (κ, exclusion of June–July observations, probe-only labels, alternative anchors, inputs without the CCI product, year-matched TDD). The confirmatory hypotheses are L10 (residual structure versus physics-input structure), L12 (additive versus multiplicative correction), L15 (Stefan pseudo-labels versus four placebo pseudo-labels), L19 (conclusions under random splits versus region holdout), L29 (existence of a physics or product baseline better than P0 at n = 0) and L30 (direct ML at n = 0 with higher-capacity learners). If L29 identifies a better baseline P*, the contrasts of L4, L8 and L10 are repeated against P*. The manuscript does not state that a learner improves on the physics baseline when it improves on P0 but not on P*. Holm-adjusted p values within each item are reported as auxiliary columns.

### Predictive uncertainty (LGU)

The uncertainty experiment evaluates predictive distributions in held-out regions. Every method outputs 99 quantiles per scoring cell. Intervals, the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS, grid approximation) and pinball losses are computed from these quantiles (Gneiting and Raftery, 2007). The interval score of a central (1 − α) interval [L, U] is

IS_α = (U − L) + (2/α)(L − y)_+ + (2/α)(y − U)_+  (cm).  (7)

Experiment A is a ladder of hierarchical predictive distributions for u = log y − log(E0 s), modelled as u = a_r + b_(r,k) + ε with a region offset a_r, a block offset b ~ N(0, τ_b²) and a cell residual ε. Hyperparameters are estimated from the source only. Each source region's offset is computed against the coefficient estimated without that region, so that source regions mimic a new target. The confirmatory stage (iii) uses a Student-t prior with 4 degrees of freedom for a_r and a half-normal hyperprior on its scale, integrated on a grid. The posterior of a_r given the n labels yields the predictive distribution in unlabelled blocks. Other stages use a normal prior, heteroscedastic residuals from a CatBoost scale model, a normalizing-flow residual distribution that is run only if it passes a registered gate, and a conformal post-correction. The main baseline B4 is centred on E_n s with a constant log half-width. B4 is calibrated by treating each source region in turn as a pseudo-target with the same draw procedure. Experiment B compares normalizers of a hierarchical conformal interval at n = 0: constant width, normalizing flow, CatBoost multi-quantile regression, conditional flow matching and a physics-propagated width. Each normalizer is compared with a placebo that permutes it among cells. Calibration groups are source macro-regions with at least 20 cells, pooled with equal weight (Dunn et al., 2023). Experiment C reports spatial-information diagnostics without hypotheses.

Confidence intervals use a two-stage bootstrap. Blocks are resampled within macro-regions on both the calibration side and the scoring side, and the calibration quantities are recomputed in each replicate. The registration specified 2,000 replicates and a timing rule for reducing them; the rule was applied before the main run and set the count to 1,000. The equivalence margin for interval scores is 5 % of the baseline score (auxiliary 10 %). If the half-width of an interval exceeds the margin, equivalence is recorded as not testable. A lower interval score is used as support only if the 90 % coverage of the method is at least 0.80. The confirmatory hypotheses are LGU-A1 (stage (iii) versus B4 at n ∈ {10, 40}, pooled over the Lena River Delta, Canada and Alaska) and LGU-B2 (flow normalizer versus its placebo at n = 0 in the four main regions). A statement that conditional interval width transfers between regions is made only if LGU-B2 meets its four registered conditions. With two to four calibration groups there is no finite-sample coverage guarantee, and coverage is reported as an empirical quantity.

### Tabular foundation models and neural-network tuning (LGT, LGF)

TabPFN v2 (Hollmann et al., 2025; package tabpfn 8.0.7 with the public v2 regression weights) and TabICL v2 (Qu et al., 2026; package tabicl 2.0.2) were run on the same 27 targets, splits and draws with default settings and without fine-tuning. Both receive a context of at most 10,000 rows, the pre-training range of TabPFN v2. All n target labels enter the context. Source rows fill the remaining budget of 10,000 − max(T, 1,000) rows, where T is the number of target rows, by a region-proportional nested subsample. The source context is therefore identical for all n ≤ 1,000. E0 is always computed from the full source set. Each foundation model is paired with CatBoost fitted on the identical context in the same process. TabICL is additionally run with the full source set as context. The methods are D0, R0 and R1. The augmentation methods (D1, R2, R3) are omitted because the context limit does not allow the pseudo-label ratio of the main experiment.

The tuning experiment re-selects the hyperparameters of the MLP, the multi-head MLP, the reduced FT-Transformer-type model and RealMLP using source data only. For each target and learner, a random search (Bergstra and Bengio, 2012) over up to 34 configurations including the default is scored by leave-one-source-region-out cross-validation with equal region weight [verify: configuration counts per learner after LGF revision 5]. The best candidates are refitted with two new seeds. A non-default configuration is selected only if it improves the fold-wise score consistently over the default, which limits selection bias (Cawley and Talbot, 2010). Target labels and scoring cells are never used for selection. Default and tuned versions are both refitted on the local server, so that each comparison is closed on one platform. The confirmatory hypotheses are LGF-F1 (TabICL direct prediction at n = 0 under both context conditions versus P0) and LGF-N1 (tuned direct prediction of each of the four neural learners at n = 0 versus P0). Both hypotheses have the form "not superior". Support is split into two classes: inferior or equivalent in all contrasts, and no evidence of superiority when a contrast is indeterminate. Only the first class enters statements that a conclusion holds across learner types. Statements about tabular foundation models as a class are limited to the two models tested.
